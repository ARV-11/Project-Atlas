# Driver Persona — Richard

> **Project:** Project Atlas  
> **Persona:** Richard  
> **User Type:** Part-Time Ride-Share Driver  
> **Persona Status:** Simulated / Research-Based  
> **Phase:** Discovery & Vision  
> **Version:** 1.0

---

## 1. Persona Overview

Richard represents a segment of part-time ride-share drivers who use ride-sharing as a supplementary source of income alongside their primary employment.

He is motivated primarily by improving his household's financial position while maintaining sufficient time for his family and personal responsibilities.

Richard is comfortable using mobile applications and regularly compares different ride-sharing platforms to identify opportunities that provide better financial returns.

---

## 2. Demographic Profile

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| Name | Richard |
| Age | 35 |
| Location | Melbourne, Australia |
| Family | Married, father of two |
| Primary Employment | Disability sector |
| Ride-Share Employment | Part-time |
| Ride-Share Frequency | 3–4 days per week |
| Typical Driving Session | Approximately 3–5 hours |
| Digital Literacy | Comfortable with mobile applications |

---

## 3. Background

Richard works full-time in the disability sector, typically finishing his primary employment at approximately 4:00 PM.

After returning home, he prioritises spending time with his family and supporting household responsibilities.

He generally begins ride-sharing around 8:00 PM when he has sufficient time and energy.

Ride-sharing therefore needs to fit around his existing employment and family commitments rather than functioning as his sole source of income.

---

## 4. Goals

### Primary Goals

- Earn approximately $1,000 per week from ride-sharing.
- Generate approximately $4,000 per month from ride-sharing.
- Increase household financial security.
- Save and invest surplus income.
- Maintain sufficient time with his family.

### Secondary Goals

- Build professional and social connections through interactions with passengers.
- Maximise earnings during periods of high demand.
- Select opportunities that provide the best return for his available driving time.

---

## 5. Motivations

Richard's primary motivation for ride-sharing is additional income.

His financial priorities include:

- Children's education expenses
- Household expenses
- Rent
- Savings
- Investments
- Long-term family financial security

However, financial gain is not his only motivation.

Richard also enjoys interacting with passengers and expanding his personal and professional network.

---

## 6. Pain Points

### Primary Pain Point

Richard believes that the compensation received for individual rides does not always adequately reflect the time invested in completing them.

He is particularly conscious of the relationship between:

> **Time spent driving → Revenue generated → Actual financial benefit**

### Secondary Pain Points

- Platform commissions reduce effective earnings.
- Rising operating and living costs reduce disposable income.
- Achieving his weekly income target may require significant driving time.
- Balancing ride-sharing with employment and family commitments can be difficult.
- He may need to selectively drive during high-demand periods to maximise income.

---

## 7. Current Behaviour

Richard currently:

- Drives approximately 3–4 days per week.
- Typically drives for 3–5 hours at a time.
- Targets approximately $1,000 in weekly ride-share earnings.
- Monitors platform bonuses and incentives.
- Looks for high-demand periods.
- Considers weather conditions when deciding when to drive.
- Compares different ride-sharing platforms.
- Reviews his earnings against the time spent driving.

---

## 8. Decision-Making Behaviour

Richard evaluates ride-sharing opportunities primarily through an economic lens.

After completing his driving activities, one of the first things he considers is:

> **"How much did I earn relative to the time I spent driving?"**

He pays particular attention to:

- Earnings per ride
- Earnings per hour
- Bonuses
- Ride frequency
- Demand conditions
- Platform commission
- Overall take-home income

---

## 9. Technology Behaviour

Richard is comfortable using mobile applications.

His existing digital behaviour includes:

- Social media applications
- Food delivery applications
- News applications
- Ride-sharing applications
- Comparing digital services

This suggests that a mobile-first ride-sharing experience is unlikely to represent a significant usability barrier for this persona.

## 10. Emotional Drivers

### What makes Richard happy?

- Financial security for his family
- Seeing his savings grow
- Being able to invest
- Spending quality time with his children
- Maintaining a healthy balance between work and family

### What causes frustration?

- Low effective earnings
- Spending significant time driving for insufficient financial return
- Difficulty managing his available time
- Feeling that the effort invested in individual rides is not adequately rewarded

---

## 11. Switching Triggers

Richard is likely to consider switching platforms if another service provides:

1. Higher ride frequency
2. Better earnings per ride
3. Higher effective hourly earnings
4. More attractive incentives
5. Better transparency around earnings

A platform that can demonstrate a clear financial advantage may therefore have a strong opportunity to attract this user segment.

---

## 12. User Needs

Based on the persona analysis, Richard requires:

### Financial Transparency

He needs to understand how much he is earning relative to the time he spends driving.

### Earning Potential

He wants to maximise the income generated during his limited driving hours.

### Flexibility

The platform must allow him to drive when it fits his existing employment and family commitments.

### Demand Visibility

Information about demand and high-value periods can help him decide when to drive.

### Predictable Platform Costs

A predictable pricing model may be attractive if it allows him to retain more of his ride revenue.

### Platform Choice

He needs confidence that switching platforms will produce a meaningful financial benefit.

---

## 14. Persona Quote

> **"If I'm going to spend my limited free time driving, I want to know that the money I'm making is actually worth the time I'm putting in."**

---

## 15. Key Insights

### Insight 1 — Richard optimises for income per unit of time.

The amount earned is important, but the relationship between earnings and driving time is more significant.

### Insight 2 — Richard is not a full-time driver.

His platform experience must therefore support flexible participation.

### Insight 3 — Family time has significant value.

Increasing earnings by requiring substantially more driving may not represent a successful solution.

### Insight 4 — Richard is economically sensitive.

Subscription pricing must provide a clear financial advantage compared with existing alternatives.

### Insight 5 — Richard actively compares platforms.

Switching barriers may therefore be lower if Atlas demonstrates superior economic value.

---

## 16. Product Design Implications

The persona suggests that Atlas should prioritise:

- Transparent earnings information
- Net earnings calculations
- Earnings-per-hour metrics
- Subscription cost visibility
- Demand indicators
- Flexible driver availability
- Incentive transparency
- Simple mobile interaction

These implications will inform the requirements and future-state design phases.

---

## 17. Persona Limitations

This persona is based on a simulated discovery scenario and should not be interpreted as statistically representative of Australian ride-share drivers.

Further validation should be conducted through:

- Driver interviews
- Surveys
- Competitor research
- Market research
- Behavioural data
- Usability testing

The persona should be updated if new evidence contradicts the assumptions currently represented.
