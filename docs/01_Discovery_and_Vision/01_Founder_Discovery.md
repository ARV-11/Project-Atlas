## 1. Executive Summary

Project Atlas is a proposed ride-sharing platform designed around a driver-first subscription business model.

Unlike traditional ride-sharing platforms that primarily generate revenue by taking a commission from individual rides, Project Atlas proposes charging drivers a predictable monthly subscription fee while allowing them to retain a significantly greater proportion of their ride earnings.

The initiative is driven by a perceived gap between the income generated by ride-share drivers and the increasing cost of living and operating a vehicle in Australia. Drivers may undertake ride-sharing as a primary or supplementary source of income, yet fuel costs, vehicle expenses, platform commissions, and other living expenses can significantly reduce their effective earnings.

Project Atlas aims to investigate whether an alternative commercial model can provide drivers with improved earning potential and greater income predictability while establishing a financially sustainable ride-sharing marketplace.

The project will initially focus on understanding driver needs, validating the business opportunity, analysing the Australian ride-sharing market, defining the required product capabilities, and evaluating the commercial and technical feasibility of the proposed solution.

# 2. Business Context

The Australian ride-sharing market provides flexible income opportunities for drivers and convenient transportation options for passengers.

However, drivers operate within a cost-intensive environment. Their effective income is influenced by factors including:

- Platform commissions
- Fuel costs
- Vehicle maintenance
- Insurance
- Registration
- Depreciation
- Travel time between rides
- Demand fluctuations
- Personal and household expenses

For drivers who use ride-sharing as a supplementary income source, the relationship between time invested and net income is particularly important.

Project Atlas proposes investigating whether a subscription-based platform can create a more attractive economic proposition for drivers by replacing or substantially reducing per-ride commission charges.

---

# 3. Business Vision

### Vision Statement

> **To build a driver-first mobility platform that enables ride-share drivers to retain more of their earnings while providing riders with a reliable, competitive and convenient transportation service.**

Project Atlas aims to explore a ride-sharing ecosystem in which the commercial relationship between the platform and drivers is based on predictable subscription pricing rather than continuous deductions from individual rides.

The long-term vision is to create a sustainable two-sided marketplace that provides value to both drivers and riders.

---

# 4. Business Problem

## 4.1 Problem Statement

Ride-share drivers may experience reduced effective earnings because a portion of each completed ride is retained by the platform while drivers remain responsible for vehicle operating costs and other personal expenses.

As operating and living costs increase, drivers may need to complete more rides to achieve their desired income.

This can create a tension between:

- Income generation
- Time spent driving
- Vehicle operating costs
- Personal and family commitments
- Financial security

Project Atlas seeks to investigate whether a subscription-based commercial model can improve this relationship.

---

# 5. Opportunity Statement

There is an opportunity to investigate an alternative ride-sharing business model in which drivers pay a predictable recurring subscription fee rather than surrendering a significant percentage of each ride's value to the platform.

If the model is economically viable, it could potentially:

- Increase driver take-home earnings
- Improve income predictability
- Reduce the number of rides required to achieve an income target
- Improve driver retention
- Create stronger alignment between platform success and driver success
- Provide a differentiated market position

However, these benefits are currently hypotheses and require validation through market research, financial modelling and user research.
