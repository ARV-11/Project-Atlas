## 1. Persona Overview

Emily represents an urban customer who uses ride-sharing services when convenient, reliable and affordable transportation is required.

She values predictable pricing, short waiting times, safety and a smooth digital booking experience.

Unlike the driver persona, Emily is primarily concerned with the quality and convenience of the transportation service rather than the platform's underlying driver compensation model.

However, driver availability and satisfaction indirectly influence her experience because insufficient driver supply can result in longer waiting times, higher prices and reduced ride availability.

# 3. Background

Emily lives and works in Melbourne and uses a combination of public transport, walking and ride-sharing to travel around the city.

She generally chooses ride-sharing when public transport is inconvenient, unavailable, too slow or unsuitable for the circumstances of her journey.

Ride-sharing may also be used during late-night travel, adverse weather conditions, social occasions or when carrying items makes public transport inconvenient.

# 15. Key Insights

### Insight 1 — Rider value is primarily experience-driven.

Unlike drivers, riders do not primarily evaluate the platform based on the platform's revenue model.

Their experience is driven by price, availability, reliability and safety.

### Insight 2 — Driver economics indirectly affect riders.

If the platform cannot attract and retain enough drivers, rider availability may decline.

Therefore:

> **Driver value → Driver supply → Rider availability → Rider experience**

### Insight 3 — Competitive pricing remains important.

A driver-first business model cannot succeed if its commercial structure results in fares that riders perceive as uncompetitive.

### Insight 4 — Reliability is critical.

A low-cost ride is unlikely to compensate for excessive waiting times or frequent cancellations.

### Insight 5 — Trust is essential.

Safety, driver information, transparent pricing and reliable communication contribute to rider confidence.

---

# 16. Product Design Implications

The rider persona suggests that Atlas should prioritise:

- Simple ride booking
- Upfront fare visibility
- Accurate estimated arrival times
- Real-time driver tracking
- Driver identification
- Safety features
- Digital payments
- Ride history
- Ratings and reviews
- Customer support
- Reliable driver availability

These implications will be validated and refined during later requirements and solution-design activities.
